About

I am a Senior Researcher at Ericsson Research in Stockholm, Sweden, in the Sensing and Perception team. My research is on semantic perception for mobile robots: how a robot builds an understanding of its surroundings from camera data, and how that understanding can stay reliable as the robot moves into places and conditions it was not trained for.

My research interests include domain adaptation and test-time adaptation for semantic segmentation, domain shift detection, open-vocabulary perception with vision foundation models, and efficient on-device deployment of perception models on embedded platforms. More broadly I am interested in semantic mapping and navigation, scene understanding and the link between perception and robot decision making.

Since 2025 I work on Seamless indoor-outdoor mobile robot semantic perception and navigation with domain adaptation (SIMNAV), a Digital Futures Industrial Innovation Project between Ericsson Research and KTH Royal Institute of Technology. The project targets mobile robots, such as delivery robots, that operate mostly outdoors but must also work in varied indoor environments, and aims at fast and scalable adaptation of segmentation models that is practical on the robot itself. Collaboration with academia is a constant in my work, through joint projects, shared publications, co-supervision of students and guest lecturing.

Before Ericsson I obtained my PhD at Universidad Carlos III de Madrid (2021, cum laude, international doctorate mention) in the RoboticsLab under Ramón Barber, with the thesis Scene Understanding for Autonomous Robots Operating in Indoor Environments. That work covered semantic and topological navigation, object-based localisation and place recognition for indoor service robots, and included research visits to DLR, CIIRC at CTU Prague and the AASS lab at Örebro University. I also taught mobile robotics and industrial automation at UC3M for five years.

Research

Training-free adaptation

Adapting open-vocabulary segmentation models to new domains without back-propagation, using a library of lightweight adapters indexed by vision-language embeddings.

Knowing when to adapt

Multi-signal domain shift detection that fuses visual, adapter-drift and semantic signals to trigger adaptation only when the environment really changes.

Perception at the edge

Efficient inference, offloading and resource-aware tracking so that adaptive perception runs on robots and embedded devices connected over 5G and 6G networks.
